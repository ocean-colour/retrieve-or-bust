# The Week-1 elastic RT prototype — what it is, what it shows, what it does not

**Branch `rt-elastic-prototype` · milestones M0–M4 · 2026-08-07**

A differentiable forward model for elastic remote-sensing reflectance,
`Rrs = Rrs_ZTT + ΔRrs`: an analytic radiative-transfer backbone with a small learned
correction, in JAX, end-to-end differentiable, trained and scored on Loisel+2023.

This document is the summary for review. The full account is
[`rt_elastic_implementation.md`](rt_elastic_implementation.md) (v0.16); the numbers are
regenerated by [`py/run_validation.py`](py/run_validation.py) into
[`validation/`](validation/).

## The headline numbers

Full 9960-sample L23 batch, rRMS in `rrs` space (design §6), held out **by scene**:

| model | train | held-out scenes | held-out @60° |
|---|---|---|---|
| standard Gordon (1988) | 7.21 | 7.21 | 9.01 |
| ZTT backbone alone | 5.95 | 5.93 | 8.11 |
| **O25 form, refit on L23** (12 fitted numbers) | **0.70** | **0.69** | **0.71** |
| hybrid, linear emulator (8) | 2.57 | 2.54 | 2.48 |
| **hybrid, MLP emulator** (417) | **0.30** | **0.30** | **0.32** |

Held-out equals train to two decimals, so the gain is generalisation rather than fit.
Speed: the hybrid runs the full batch in ~15 ms, 4.5–6× the backbone. Gradients agree
with central differences to ≤5e-9 for every model and every input.

## What the prototype may claim

On held-out water bodies it reaches **0.30% rRMS**, uniform across the spectrum and
across the three solar zeniths, differentiable in every input, at ~5× the cost of the
analytic backbone alone.

## What it may not claim — read this before quoting the number above

1. **The margin over the state of the art is 2.3×, not 24×.** Against standard Gordon
   the hybrid is 24× better; against **O25**, the modern benchmark, it is 2.3×. Quoting
   the first without the second would be comparing against a 1988 model.
2. **O25's own 0.69% is its best case.** Its coefficients are not the published ones —
   the paper prints only plots — so they were refit on *our* training split with *our*
   metric as the objective. Every table says "O25 form, refit on L23". The fitting
   objective matters: the paper's unweighted least squares would have scored 2.6%, and
   using it would have flattered our hybrid by ~4×.
3. **Geometry extrapolation is unresolved, and this is the main open risk.** Trained on
   0°/30° and asked for an unseen 60°, the hybrid scores **4.74 / 8.37 / 7.75 / 5.40 /
   12.24%** across five random seeds — the answer depends on the seed, and the band
   straddles Gordon's 9.01%. The refit O25 gets **4.63% deterministically** there. So
   **at an unseen geometry the benchmark beats us and is more reproducible.** The
   milestone's gate is written on the scene split for this reason; the zenith split is
   reported, not gated (JXP's call).
4. **Nothing here tests phase-function generalisation.** L23 spans a factor 1.7 in `B_p`
   against the design's ~7× band, and a single fixed Fournier-Forand shape. The flat
   per-`B_p`-bin accuracy is *not* evidence; it says only that accuracy is even across a
   narrow slice.
5. **Nadir only.** L23 fixes the sensor at nadir and the azimuth at zero, so the BRDF
   axes are untested. The emulator's domain check now flags **any** off-nadir view —
   which is correct, and means the hybrid falls back to the backbone there under
   `on_out_of_domain="ztt"`.
6. **ZTT's µ∞ is from Twardowski & Tonizzo (2017)**, because the 2018 paper's Equation
   (8) coefficients are not published. Report as *ZTT with the TT2017 µ∞*.

## The acceptance gate, as amended

The coding plan asked for "hybrid beats standard Gordon on **both** held-out splits, and
passes the gradient gate". Two amendments were made deliberately, both recorded:

- **Gate on beating O25 on the scene split**, not Gordon (Gordon is the weakest model in
  the table, and ZTT alone already beats it, so an emulator returning exactly zero would
  have passed). Gordon and ZTT are retained as the floor.
- **Report the zenith split rather than gating it**, because the outcome is
  seed-dependent (item 3 above). Note the interaction: the out-of-domain fallback added
  for this situation deliberately does *not* fire at 60°, since the sanctioned envelope
  reaches 60°, so it cannot rescue that half.

By the amended gate, the prototype **passes**: the hybrid beats O25 on held-out scenes
with a margin, beats Gordon and the backbone, and clears the gradient gate.


Both writers verify before replacing anything, and the training run is bit-identical
between runs.

## Known defects fixed during review, for the reviewer's confidence

Four review passes (PRs #9–#12 plus a self-review) found and fixed: an off-nadir view
that passed the domain check while the emulator's output was meaningless; two
implementations of one predicate disagreeing on NaN; a shipped-weights path that could
have written the wrong model; malformed committed CSVs; a stale committed figure; and
four tests that could not fail. Each fix is pinned by a regression test demonstrated to
fail when the fix is reverted. See §5.6b and §6.9 of the record.
